About

I spent eleven years in publishing before I left — not in a single dramatic moment, but slowly, the way most real changes happen. It took me two years to actually leave once I knew I wanted to.

That's the part nobody prepares you for: knowing isn't the same as moving. I trained as a coach specifically to work with that gap — the distance between recognizing something isn't working and actually rebuilding your life around a different answer.

My clients are usually 35 to 55, often successful by every visible measure, and quietly certain that the current version of their life isn't the one they want to keep living. Some are leaving careers. Some are leaving relationships, cities, or identities they've outgrown. I don't hand out five-step plans. I ask better questions, hold you to what you actually say you want, and help you build a plan you can stand behind on the hard days.

How it works

Four stages, not a fixed script

Every engagement moves through the same rough terrain, though the pace is entirely yours. Some people move through all four in a season. Others spend a year in stage two.

1

Take stock

The first two sessions are mostly listening. We map what's actually true about your situation — separating what you're afraid of from what's real, and what's habit from what's actually necessary.

2

Name the want

Most people arrive knowing what they don't want. This stage is slower and harder: getting specific about what you're actually moving toward, without borrowing someone else's definition of a good life.

3

Build the bridge

We turn the direction into an actual plan — financial runway, timing, the conversations you've been avoiding, and the smaller decisions that make the big one possible.

4

Hold the line

Change rarely fails at the decision — it fails in month four, when it gets hard and quiet. This stage is about staying accountable to yourself once the initial momentum fades.
